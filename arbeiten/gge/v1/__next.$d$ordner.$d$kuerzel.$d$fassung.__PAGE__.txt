75:T415,Here Hume lets the historian or annalist serve as the example in which the connection of ideas that are contiguous in space and time can be grasped as primary. A treatise on the French Revolution would probably resemble the ravings of a madman if it constantly described events in Thailand around 1600. The spatial and temporal unity of ideas thus forms a connection the understanding can comprehend. We could argue that contiguity in space and time can be understood as a subcategory of resemblance, since the chronicler’s events must resemble one another in temporal order and spatial position in order to form a unity.
